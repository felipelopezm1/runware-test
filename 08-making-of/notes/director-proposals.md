# Director's proposals: where a shot is likely to fail, and one change for each

Storyboard `is-this-all-on-runware`, rev 10, 2026-11-19. By the director spider, for Felipe's yes or no.

**None of this is applied.** The storyboard is faithful to the pack: the script, the three shot lists and the
bible, shot for shot. Nothing was generated, so every risk below is a judgment from the film grammar, the
director's review, the numbers in the pack and what the zombie test showed, not from a failed take of this
film. Three things are fixed and no proposal touches them: the story and its ending, each character's LOCK
BLOCK, and the final line.

**Prices.** Seedance 2.5 on Runware, from the quartermaster's `runware-facts.md` beside this file: $0.614 a
second at 1080p (what Runware charged us on 2026-11-11) and $0.921 a second at 4K (listed, never yet
charged). Its shortest take is 4 s, so a shot cut into two pieces costs 8 s of generation however short the
pieces are on screen. A Nano Banana Pro frame is about $0.14.

## The one fact behind most of them: the words

The script holds 40 lines and 176 words (the table at its foot says 47 and 213). Said one after another at an
ordinary 2.6 words a second that is about 68 s of speech in a 60 s film. It fits only the way the WORLD block
asks for it: "overlapping, half-heard under the action". A model that speaks on the clip does not overlap
three voices on cue; a mix does. So for each shot the proposal says which line Seedance performs on camera
(one face, readable) and which are laid in from the voices that step 10 of the master prompt records anyway.

## Shot by shot

### 1A. The last of the bread
- **Risk.** Seven faces in one wide frame and four people saying 30 words in 5 s: a wide cannot show whose
  mouth is whose, and the words do not fit.
- **Evidence.** 6.0 words a second; said in turn the four lines take about 11.5 s. Review: few-faces (7),
  one-idea (14 counted). Four acts in a row for two of the seven (Vessa cuts and hands, Borrin weighs and
  drinks) while five others idle.
- **Recommendation.** Seedance speaks no line in 1A. Ask it for the picture and the room (quiet talk, mouths
  moving, half heard) and lay the four recorded lines in the cut, overlapping as the script says, with Vel's
  "Two days of food. Keep your voices down." running over the cut into the first second of 1B.
- **Cost.** Nothing extra: the four lines are recorded anyway. Seedance's own speech on this clip is given up.

### 1B. The dead pond
- **Risk.** 19 words in 4 s on a close two-shot, 16 of them inside the first 2 s, then a rack of focus, three
  barrel events and a third speaker in the last 2 s.
- **Evidence.** 4.8 words a second; Oswin's murmur alone is about 2.7 s and Borrin's line about 3.5 s. Eight
  events. Review: one-idea (9); its lip-sync warning is a false alarm about the engine and a true one about
  the time.
- **Recommendation.** Seedance performs one line on camera, Borrin's, starting at 0.3 s and running on over
  the rack so its last words are heard over the black water. Oswin's murmur is laid in from the recording,
  low, beginning under the last second of 1A, where his lips already move. Sylwen's "The barrel moved." is
  laid in at 3 s over the barrel: she is soft and out of focus, with no lips to match.
- **Cost.** Nothing extra.

### 1C. Company (Dax's POV)
- **Risk.** One unbroken handheld take that looks up the stairs, swings left across the room to the barrels
  and three people, swings back and jumps onto the stairs with Dax's own forearms in frame: about ten events
  and five speakers in 6 s.
- **Evidence.** The bible's own camera rules: "One main action a shot", "Never two moves". The body asks for a
  float back, a swing left, a swing back and a leap. A point of view with the character's own arms and a jump
  is among the hardest things to hold. Review: one-idea (11), few-faces (7). In the zombie test Seedance cut
  inside its takes on its own (at 5.75 s and at 8.5 s): a take that must "never cut" is the one it cuts.
- **Recommendation.** Two takes in the same 6 s, both from Dax's eyes, so the one cut falls where we choose.
  1C-1 (0 to 2.5 s), the stairs: the eyes, Mordecai's hand and "Company.", the frogs pouring toward the lens,
  the camera only floating back. 1C-2 (2.5 to 6 s) starts on the barrels bursting at the left and pans right
  across Vel ("Lantern up. Line."), Borrin's rising lantern and Vessa ("FINALLY.") back to the stairs, ending
  on the party starting up the steps through the frogs. Dax's forearms, half sword and leap are left out of
  the picture: he is the camera, and "One!" and Oswin's line are heard, not seen.
- **Cost.** 8 s generated for 6 s on screen: 2 s more a round ($1.23 at 1080p, $1.84 at 4K), and one more
  frame (the barrels, $0.14).

### 2A. Eleven. Nine.
- **Risk.** Two set pieces in 5 s behind an over-shoulder follow: Mordecai's ring of fire with the three-line
  count, then Dax's leap over the barrels, the chop and the skid.
- **Evidence.** About nine events. The grammar rates over-shoulder follow 3 of 5 for a video model, and the
  review says so. Review: one-idea (11), few-faces (8, though here they are backs and a shoulder).
- **Recommendation.** Two takes on the same shoulder, cut at the body's own 2 s mark. 2A-1 (0 to 2 s): the
  ring of fire, Vel's lunge, the count. 2A-2 (2 to 5 s): Dax's leap, chop and skid and Vessa's answer, started
  on 2A-1's last frame so the camera, the light and every position carry exactly. All five lines are laid in:
  every speaker has their back to the camera or is in the air.
- **Cost.** 8 s generated for 5 s on screen: 3 s more a round ($1.84 at 1080p, $2.76 at 4K). No extra frame.

### 2B. Amphibians
- **Risk.** Four characters with a gag each and 16 words in 4 s: second for second the most crowded shot of
  the film.
- **Evidence.** About nine events; 4.0 words a second (6.2 s of speech). Review: one-idea (7), few-faces (5).
  The body ties Sylwen's bandage "with her teeth" in the same breath as her line.
- **Recommendation.** Seedance performs Oswin's line only. His cast, the wave and the unimpressed frogs are
  the shot's one main action; Vessa's fall behind him is its one small one. Sylwen's and Borrin's lines are
  laid in as she walks past and he head-butts the toad at the frame's edge, Borrin's toast running half a
  second over the cut into 2C, whose first 2 s have no words.
- **Cost.** Nothing extra.

### 2C. They're running
- **Risk.** Four lines are written into one 2 s window, and the 2 s after it are silent.
- **Evidence.** 10 words between 2 and 4 s is 5 words a second, with a head turn between Vel's two lines.
  Review: few-faces (8, seven of them profiles in a wide), one-idea (9). Parallel track is rated 2 of 5: fine.
- **Recommendation.** Spread the four lines over 2 to 6 s: Vel's two on camera between 2 and 4 s as written;
  Borrin's "Told you. Pond." and Mordecai's "Oh, good." laid in under her walk to the archway, between 4 and
  5.5 s, so the silhouette still lands in silence.
- **Cost.** Nothing extra.

### 3A. Nobody on the mud
- No proposal. The seven are seen from behind, the rise is a move video models hold (2 of 5), and nine words
  in 5 s fit. It is the safest shot of scene 3.

### 3B. The scream (Borrin's POV)
- **Risk.** 24 words from three speakers inside the last 2 s, under the loudest sound in the film.
- **Evidence.** 12 words a second in the body's "3 to 5 s". Oswin's prayer alone is 13 words. No mouth is in
  the picture: the speakers are a hand at the right edge, two arms at the left edge and an arm crossing the
  frame. The body has Vel shout; the bible says she never raises her voice.
- **Recommendation.** Seedance speaks no line in 3B. The three recorded lines are laid in: Oswin's prayer from
  1 s, under the eruption and the scream; Mordecai from 2.6 s; Vel's four words at 4 s, hard and clipped
  rather than shouted.
- **Cost.** Nothing extra.

### 3C. It's soft
- **Risk.** Two ideas in one 5 s handheld take: the attack that goes wrong (hack, laugh, sweep, slam, the coil
  closing round Sylwen) and the heal from inside the coil (wave, Oswin, palms, the wound closing).
- **Evidence.** About seven events; 3.4 words a second from three speakers. A creature wrapping a person, a
  wave of water and a glow in one clip are what video models hold worst. Review: one-idea (10).
- **Recommendation.** Two takes cut at the body's own 2 s mark. 3C-1 (0 to 2 s): Vessa at the collar, "It's
  SOFT. Why is it SOFT.", the sweep that flattens her and takes Sylwen. 3C-2 (2 to 5 s), held on the two
  women: the wave from the left with Oswin's "It worked. It WORKED." heard from off frame, then Sylwen's
  palms, the wound closing and her line on camera.
- **Cost.** 8 s generated for 5 s on screen: 3 s more a round ($1.84 at 1080p, $2.76 at 4K), and one more
  frame (the two women, $0.14).

### 3D. Eight
- **Risk.** The hardest shot of the film: about twelve events at two places (Dax up on the worm's head at the
  back right; Vel and Sylwen in the coil at the left) under one arc that follows Dax away from where the
  second half happens.
- **Evidence.** Arc follow is the hardest move the pack names (4 of 5; the grammar asks for start and end
  frames or stitched clips at that level) and the review says so. Review: one-idea (10). The body's camera
  follows Dax "to the mud" while its last 3 s are Vessa and Vel. "No cut" again, on the model that cut the
  zombie test's takes by itself.
- **Recommendation.** Two takes cut at the body's own 3 s mark. 3D-1 (0 to 3 s), the jump: over Vessa's
  shoulder, the arc with Dax up the blocks, the rain opening, "EIGHT!", the blade in, the scream, the slip
  and the slide. 3D-2 (3 to 6 s), the rescue: Vessa's "DAX!", Vel knee-deep hauling Sylwen out of the
  loosening coil, "I said. Nobody. On the mud." on Vel's face.
- **Cost.** 8 s generated for 6 s on screen: 2 s more a round ($1.23 at 1080p, $1.84 at 4K), and one more
  frame (the rescue, $0.14). At 4K each 4 s take is $3.69, under the owner's $5 line; the 6 s take is $5.52,
  over it.

### 3E. This one is mine (Vel's POV)
- **Risk.** A point of view whose owner's hands enter at the bottom of an 85 mm frame that is aimed up at
  Mordecai and has just snap-zoomed onto his skull: the lens cannot hold both.
- **Evidence.** 85 mm sees about 24 degrees. After the snap zoom the frame is a skull and a helmet; the mud at
  Vel's knees is far outside it. The table calls the framing "single" and the POV column "Vel's POV". Vel says
  her line "not looking up" from a camera that looks up. "Static, then snap-zoom-punch" is two moves, against
  the bible's "Never two moves"; the punch is rated 3 of 5 and the review says so.
- **Recommendation.** Stay on Mordecai to the end. Take out one picture only: Vel's gloved hands setting
  Sylwen down beside Dax. Her "Assist." is heard from behind the camera, because she is the camera, and
  Mordecai's skull turns down to the lens for "It is NOT an assist." Sylwen is already clear of the coil at
  the end of 3D and the bible's ladder has her kneeling over Dax, so the story loses nothing.
- **Cost.** Nothing extra.

### 3F. Is this all on Runware?
- **Risk.** The last line of the film and Vel's look are given one second.
- **Evidence.** The body's "3 to 4 s": five words, "quiet, slurred, utterly sincere", about 2 s to say, then
  the look. The script asks for a "Long beat" before it. Review: few-faces (8), though a locked-off wide is
  the kindest case there is.
- **Recommendation.** Let 3F run 6 s: 3 s of silence and drizzle, the line from 3 to 5.2 s, Vel's look to 6 s,
  then the fade. The film is then 62 s, inside the master prompt's own "60 s plus or minus 2".
- **Cost.** 2 s more a round ($1.23 at 1080p, $1.84 at 4K). The storyboard's 60.0 s becomes 62.0 s.

## Across the film

### The seams: `continuous` cannot mean "the same frame"
- **Risk.** The pack calls ten seams `continuous` and also gives every shot its own designed first frame from
  another camera. Both cannot be true, and the storyboard's planner takes the word literally: the last frame
  of the clip before becomes the first frame of the next.
- **Evidence.** Not one of the ten seams keeps its camera: a tableau to a close two-shot (1A to 1B), a close
  two-shot to Dax's eyes on the stairs (1B to 1C), Dax's eyes from below to Mordecai's shoulder from above (1C
  to 2A), and so on to 3D to 3E. The ambiances file says "The last frame of 1C ... is the first frame of 2A"
  while 2A's own Frame line is another picture. `board_plan` therefore plans 4 frames (1A, 2A, 3A, 3F) and 8
  carried last frames. The review marks 2A and 3A.
- **Recommendation.** Every shot gets its own Nano Banana Pro first frame from its own Frame line, as the
  master prompt's step 7 already says, and the last frame of the clip before rides along as one more
  reference for the state (mud, wounds, rain, who stands where), never as the first frame. On the storyboard
  the eight seams inside a scene become `emotional` (the grammar's word for a new first frame, made with the
  last frame as a reference, from a new angle) and 1C to 2A and 2C to 3A become `new-scene`. The split shots
  that keep their camera (2A-2, and 3C-2 if it holds its frame) are the only true carries.
- **Cost.** 8 more frames than the planner counts, about $1.10; no more than step 7 already assumes.

### A first frame or references: Seedance takes one or the other, so choose by the camera
- **Risk.** The master prompt's step 8 sends each clip its approved frame as the first frame, every face panel
  as a reference and the clay clip as a guide, together. Runware's Seedance 2.5 refuses that
  (`runware-facts.md`, answer 2): a call is either frame mode (first frame, an optional last frame, nothing
  else) or reference mode (up to 30 pictures and 10 clips, no locked first frame).
- **Evidence.** The quartermaster's reading of the model's schema, and the zombie test, where "first frame
  plus references" was refused and every Seedance call ended up in reference mode. A start frame on Seedance
  2.5 has never been tried by us.
- **Recommendation.** Let the camera decide. Where the camera hardly moves and the frame is the picture, use
  **frame mode**, with a last frame where the ending matters: 1A, 1B, 2B, 3C, 3E, 3F. Where the camera's path
  is the shot, use **reference mode** with the clay clip as the guide, the face panels as references and the
  approved frame riding as the first picture: 1C, 2A, 2C, 3A, 3B, 3D. The first take of 1A (frame mode) and of
  3A (reference mode) will show whether the rule holds before the rest are sent.
- **Cost.** Nothing in frame mode. In reference mode the clay clip is billed by its own length too: about a
  fifth more a take ($1.08 on a 5 s take at 4K, from `cost-model.md`).

### The prompt: its length, and where the action sits in it
- **Risk.** Assembled as the master prompt's step 9 says, the clip prompt runs from 6,875 characters (1B) to
  12,065 (3F), and the SHOT body, the only part that says what happens, begins after 81 to 88 per cent of it.
- **Evidence.** The quartermaster measured the same lengths: the models take them (50,000 characters for
  Seedance 2.5, 45,000 for Nano Banana Pro) and our `rw_video` and `rw_image` tools do not (3,000); the routes
  that carry the prompt whole are in `runware-facts.md`, section 3. Runware's own examples for this model and
  the zombie test's prompts were 1,100 to 1,900 characters. The bible's camera rules say "Action first, then
  who, then where, then camera, then light"; step 9's order is the reverse. `prompt_check`, run once on 1B's
  assembled prompt, answers "revise": it warns on length (1,226 words for an ask of 182: "the longest
  rewrites score lowest"), on counts spelled out inside the LOCK BLOCKS, and on the talk of dialogue in WORLD
  and SCENE; none of the three can be cured without touching a fixed block.
- **Recommendation.** Keep every block word for word and change only their order: the SHOT body first, then
  the LOCK BLOCKS of the cast with their state lines and tags, the location block, the SCENE block, WORLD,
  LOOK, and the hold line last. Whatever route carries the prompt, it goes whole: no block is cut to fit.
- **Cost.** Nothing. It departs from step 9's order, so it needs the owner's yes; and the owner should know
  that `prompt_check` will keep answering "revise" on these prompts for reasons the pack itself fixes.

### The cut must not write on the picture
- **Risk.** `board_cut` would put words on screen.
- **Evidence.** It burns captions of the dialogue by default, and it lays a shot's `onScreen` words at the
  bottom of that shot's own picture for its whole length (`runtime/director-board.mjs`, `cutTimeline`). The
  owner's rule: no words on screen but the end title, white serif on black.
- **Recommendation.** The storyboard carries no `onScreen` words at all. Cut with `captions: false`. The end
  title stays the master prompt's step 12: `title_render`, laid over the final black by `cut_edit` after the
  1.5 s fade. The storyboard cannot hold that fade: its fade-black is a way INTO a shot and lasts 1 s.
- **Cost.** Nothing.
